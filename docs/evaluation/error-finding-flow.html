Bill of quantities review · 22 Nov 2026

How Desvio finds errors in a bill

The app's own checks find what rules can prove and rank the rest by how reliable each rule is. The language model reads the whole bill three times, each time for one family of issues, and only what it calls certain goes on the list.

What each step is for

StepWhat it doesWhy
App's checksEvery registered check runs on the bill. A wrong sum or line total, a missing value or an exact duplicate is proven by arithmetic. Other findings are hints: wording, assumptions, odd prices, units.Certain and free; the model's own arithmetic is unreliable.
Rule reliabilityFor each rule, the share of its findings that were real faults is measured on bills with known faults. Findings are ordered by it, and rules right less than 1 time in 7 move to "Worth a look".This plain ranking beat the judge in the fresh run, 0.779 against 0.748. Below 1 in 7, one more entry lowers the score.
Whole billThe model gets the whole bill in one request, with the checks' findings under their positions.Against the split by section it made half as many claims at a higher hit rate, for about half the cost.
Three passesEach pass asks about one family of issues: numbers, price basis, text.Only these three found faults nothing else found. Missing work, risk wording and duplicates added false alarms and no faults on the practice bills.
Answer checksA suspected issue must name a position of the bill, a listed issue type, and quote that position.Removes invented positions and quotes before anyone sees them.
CertaintyThe model rates each claim as high, medium or low. Only high stays on the list.High was right 55 % of the time, medium 10 % and low 15 %.
Worth a lookFindings of weak rules and the model's uncertain claims, kept apart and not ranked.Nothing is thrown away. The Korrektor can open it when there is time.

The strict score ignores a hit when the same check also flags that row in the clean original. No answers from an earlier run were reused and no prompt knows an answer key. The last run cost 0.74 USD for 12 bills with the judge; without it the estimate is about 0.35 USD.

The judge was measured and left out. When it confirmed a check finding, 59 % were real faults, but 18 % of what it rejected were real too, which cost 27 faults. From the text alone it cannot recompute VAT or see why a price is odd. On the model's certain claims it confirmed 50 of 55, so it filtered nothing.

Blind test. B8, the held-out benchmark (6 faulty bills, 123 planted faults), was run once on 22 Nov with this design unchanged and the rule order taken only from the practice bills. The reading list scored 0.582 against 0.513 for the checks alone (strict score 0.462 against 0.404), with 24 entries per bill instead of 38. The gain carries over, at 0.07 instead of 0.10, and at a lower level than on the practice set: 49 of the 123 blind faults are not on the list.

Limits

  • The model's findings are suspicions for the Korrektor, never confirmed errors.
  • This design was one of three side lists of the fresh run and was named the best after the result was seen. P1 also helped design the new rules, so its numbers are optimistic. The blind test on B8 is the honest measure.
  • Rule reliability was learned on three of the four practice bills and tested on the fourth. On real bills it has to be measured again when bills with known faults exist.
  • The bill goes to Anthropic for the model steps. That is allowed for the test bills; a client's bill needs its own permission or a local model.
  • A finding counts as a hit when it names a fault's row, even if its reason differs.
